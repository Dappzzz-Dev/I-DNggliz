A short reading note

A night in the forest

A response to A Midsummer Night’s Dream by William Shakespeare

My favorite moment is when Oberon finally releases Titania from the spell.

In A Midsummer Night’s Dream, Oberon and Titania argue over a changeling boy. Oberon asks Puck to use a flower’s juice to make Titania fall in love with the first creature she sees. Puck also changes Bottom’s head into that of a donkey. When Titania wakes, she falls for Bottom.

In Act IV, Scene 1, Oberon sees Titania with Bottom and says he has received the boy. He then decides to end the spell: “Now I have the boy. And now I will release her.” I chose this scene because it is funny, but it also shows how Oberon’s jealousy caused a problem he later had to undo.

Oberon is the character I remember most. He is confident and clever, but his disagreement with Titania makes him act out of jealousy. Seeing him release her made me think about how his choices affect the other characters.

Sources: Daffa Farash Yulianto, Drama Observation (English assignment); William Shakespeare, A Midsummer Night’s Dream, Act III, Scene 1 and Act IV, Scene 1 (class text).
